Care Guide

Period Care Tips

Practical, evidence-based recommendations for managing cramps, daily hygiene, and comfort during your menstrual cycle.

Managing Your Menstrual Cycle

Proper period care is essential for maintaining optimal reproductive health, preventing infections, and feeling confident throughout your routine. Simple comfort habits like heat therapy and hydration can dramatically alleviate discomfort.

Key Reminder: Pain that disrupts your normal workday or school activities is not something you must simply tolerate. Monitor your symptoms and discuss severe cramps with a doctor.

Hygiene Best Practices

Essential daily standards for personal freshness and infection prevention.

Regular Changes

Change sanitary pads every 4 to 6 hours, and tampons every 4 to 8 hours, regardless of how light the flow appears.

Proper Hand Washing

Wash hands thoroughly with soap and warm water for at least 20 seconds before and after handling any menstrual products.

Intimate Genital Care

Cleanse only the external vulva with warm water. Avoid internal vaginal douching or heavily perfumed chemical washes.

Safe Disposal

Wrap used pads or tampons securely in paper or wrapper and place into a waste bin. Never flush menstrual products down toilets.

Comfort and Pain Management

Holistic techniques to soothe abdominal tension, lower back stiffness, and cramps.

Heat Therapy

Apply a heating pad or warm water bottle to your lower abdomen to relax uterine muscles and ease cramps.

Gentle Movement

Light physical activity like walking or restorative yoga releases natural endorphins that relieve discomfort.

Restorative Rest

Ensure 7 to 9 hours of uninterrupted sleep. Sleeping on your side in a fetal position helps relax pelvic muscles.

Continuous Hydration

Drink plenty of warm fluids, herbal chamomile or ginger tea, and warm water to reduce bloating and headache frequency.

When to Seek Medical Help

While menstrual discomfort is common, certain symptoms may indicate conditions like endometriosis, fibroids, or hormonal imbalances that require professional medical care.

  • Bleeding that soaks through one or more pads/tampons every hour for consecutive hours.
  • Menstrual bleeding that lasts longer than 7 days continuously.
  • Severe, debilitating pelvic pain that is not relieved by over-the-counter pain medications.
  • Fever, sudden chills, dizziness, vomiting, or fainting during tampon use (possible Toxic Shock Syndrome).
